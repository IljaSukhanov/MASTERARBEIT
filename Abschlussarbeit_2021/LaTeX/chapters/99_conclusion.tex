\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis investigated whether the spectral scale selection methodology developed in the manuscript~\cite{Baralic2026} can be applied to bandwidth selection in kernel density estimation. 


We constructed a sequence of kernel density estimates at candidate bandwidths, whose arrangement is controlled by a parameter $r$. The choices $r=1$ and $r=0$ correspond to linearly and geometrically spaced bandwidths, respectively. Pairwise divergences between these estimates are transformed into similarities to form a scale similarity matrix. Schoenberg's theorem provides conditions under which completely monotone transformations yield positive semi-definite similarity matrices. A bandwidth is then selected using the Perron vector of this matrix.


For Gaussian kernel density estimates based on a fixed sample, we show that, as the bandwidth grid becomes finer and the transformation parameter grows at an appropriate rate, the spectrally selected bandwidth approaches the set of global minimizers of a sample-dependent energy functional $\hat{Q}_r$ on the chosen bandwidth interval. This functional measures how quickly the density estimate changes with respect to a transformed bandwidth parameter. Thus the spectral method asymptotically selects a region in which the estimates change slowest.


The existence of local minima of $\hat{Q}_r$ was examined and a connection to classical bandwidth selection was established through the unique minimizer $h_{\text{AMQ},r}$ of the leading term approximation of the expected energy functional $\mathbb{E}[\hat{Q}_r]$. For the Gaussian kernel and the specific choice of $r=13/14$ this minimizer is asymptotically equivalent to the oracle bandwidth as the sample size tends to infinity.

Overall, the results identify an energy-minimization principle underlying spectral bandwidth selection and provide a first connection to classical MISE-based theory. An important question for future work is whether this connection can be extended to statistical guarantees for the sample-dependent spectral selector itself.
