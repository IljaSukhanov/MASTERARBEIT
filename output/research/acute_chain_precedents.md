# Acute chains and the endpoint maximum in Lemma 5

Research note, 5 October 2026. This note does not modify the thesis. Literature claims below are distinguished from calculations derived here.

The statement examined is [Lemma 5 in the bandwidth chapter](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/5_bandwidth_selection.tex:382), with the [appendix proof](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/9999_appendix.tex:220). Its assumption is

$$\delta_k=x_{k+1}-x_k,\qquad \langle\delta_k,\delta_\ell\rangle>0\quad(1\le k,\ell<N),$$

and its conclusion for $D_{ij}=\|x_i-x_j\|^2$ is $v_i<\max(v_1,v_N)$ for every interior coordinate of the positive Perron vector.

## 1. What is already in the literature?

**A direct precedent for the condition.** Hütter, Mao, Rigollet, and Robeva, *Estimation of Monge matrices*, Bernoulli 26(4), 3051–3080 (2020), §1.2 “Geometric interpretation,” p. 3052, equation (1.2), use $(x_{i+1}-x_i)^\top(x_{j+1}-x_j)\ge0$ for every pair of increments. They relate it to anti-Monge Gram matrices and Monge squared-distance matrices. Their spectral discussion concerns ordering through a centered Gram matrix. The condition is therefore established prior geometry; the stricter inequality and the convenient name “strictly acute chain” should not be described as a newly discovered geometric assumption. In the earlier manuscript the passage is in §1.1 “Geometric interpretation and spectral ordering,” p. 2. [Author manuscript; first posted 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.03136). [Published PDF, p. 3052](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/bernoulli/volume-26/issue-4/Estimation-of-Monge-matrices/10.3150/20-BEJ1215.pdf#page=2).

**An older spectral framework.** Fiedler, *Remarks on Monge matrices*, Mathematica Bohemica 127(1), 27–32 (2002), Theorem 2.2, characterizes Monge matrices by their adjacent mixed differences. Theorem 2.7 gives monotone eigenvectors associated with a nonpositive eigenvalue of maximum modulus for an equilibrated Monge matrix. Equilibrated means zero row and column sums. This addresses a centered matrix and a different spectral problem from the positive Perron vector of the original distance matrix. [Full paper, Theorems 2.2 and 2.7, pp. 29 and 31](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/133983/MathBohem_127-2002-1_3.pdf).

**A close precedent for endpoint maxima.** Bapat, Kalita, Nath, and Sarma, *Convex and quasiconvex functions on trees and their applications*, Linear Algebra and its Applications 533, 210–234 (2017), Corollary 3.2, prove strict convexity of the Perron vector of $f(D_T)$ for a tree distance matrix $D_T$ and strictly increasing convex $f$, applied entrywise. For a path and $f(t)=t^2$, this gives the endpoint conclusion for $D_{ij}=(i-j)^2$; the latter specialization is our inference. It is a definite special-case precedent, rather than a theorem covering arbitrary acute Hilbert-space chains. [Full paper, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2, p. 216](https://luit.tezu.ernet.in/naac2/Criteria-3/3.7.1/Relevant_Documents/78.pdf). [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2017.07.012).

**Assessment of novelty.** I did not locate an exact prior statement of the full acute-chain endpoint lemma in the sources checked. That does not establish novelty. A responsible presentation cites the known condition and related spectral results, then provides the endpoint proof independently. The Hilbert-space formulation itself is not a finite-dimensional novelty: after translating $x_1$ to zero, these finitely many points span a space of dimension at most $N-1$. This last observation is a direct linear-algebra derivation.

## 2. Geometric and matrix interpretation

The following are direct derivations from the stated assumption, rather than additional results attributed to the papers above.

The condition compares **all forward increments**, including increments far apart in the chain. It means that these directions make pairwise angles smaller than $90^\circ$. Checking only neighboring increments would be a weaker assumption. It permits a curved chain; collinearity is unnecessary.

Define the cone generated by the increments:

$$C=\left\{\sum_k c_k\delta_k:c_k\ge0\right\}.$$

For nonzero $u,w\in C$, bilinearity gives $\langle u,w\rangle>0$. Every forward chord belongs to $C$, since $x_j-x_i=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\delta_k$ for $i<j$. Thus forward changes remain compatible across the whole chain. With $u=\sum_k\delta_k$, one also has $\langle u,\delta_k\rangle>0$, so projection onto this direction orders the points strictly.

“Acute” refers to the forward vectors, not to every triangle formed by the points. At an ordered middle point $x_j$, for $i<j<k$,

$$\langle x_i-x_j,x_k-x_j\rangle=-\langle x_j-x_i,x_k-x_j\rangle<0.$$

The angle at the middle point is therefore obtuse when the three points are noncollinear.

The exact matrix identity is

$$D_{k,\ell}+D_{k+1,\ell+1}-D_{k,\ell+1}-D_{k+1,\ell}=-2\langle\delta_k,\delta_\ell\rangle.$$

Summing adjacent mixed differences shows that the weak condition $\langle\delta_k,\delta_\ell\rangle\ge0$ is equivalent to the Monge property for these squared distances. Strict positivity gives strict Monge inequalities whenever both index pairs are strictly ordered. The Monge convention used here is $D_{ij}+D_{k\ell}\le D_{i\ell}+D_{kj}$ for $i<k$, $j<\ell$, as in [Fiedler, definition (1) and Theorem 2.2](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/133983/MathBohem_127-2002-1_3.pdf).

For $i<j<k$, expansion gives the stronger-than-Robinson inequality

$$D_{ik}=D_{ij}+D_{jk}+2\langle x_j-x_i,x_k-x_j\rangle>D_{ij}+D_{jk}.$$

Consequently $D_{ik}>\max(D_{ij},D_{jk})$. This is the same expansion used in the [Gaussian-KDE proposition](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/5_bandwidth_selection.tex:421).

There is also a related curve interpretation. A self-contracted curve satisfies $\|\gamma(t_1)-\gamma(t_3)\|\ge\|\gamma(t_2)-\gamma(t_3)\|$ for $t_1\le t_2\le t_3$; see [Daniilidis, Ley, and Sabourau, Definition 1.2, p. 3 of the manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.0150). The polygonal interpolation of an acute chain satisfies this inequality in both time orientations, by the forward-chord calculation above. This is a consequence and a connection to broader curve geometry, not an equivalent formulation of the increment assumption.

## 3. What the Perron vector measures

Normalize $\sum_jv_j=1$ and put $\mu=\sum_jv_jx_j$, $\sigma^2=\sum_jv_j\|x_j-\mu\|^2$. The [variance decomposition in the appendix](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/9999_appendix.tex:224) gives

$$\rho v_i=\sum_jv_j\|x_i-x_j\|^2=\|x_i-\mu\|^2+\sigma^2.$$

Thus $v_i>v_j$ if and only if $\|x_i-\mu\|>\|x_j-\mu\|$. The Perron vector ranks distance from its **own weighted barycenter**, rather than distance from an externally chosen center. This is a self-consistent measure of extremeness: a large entry means large total dissimilarity to other points, weighted by their entries.

The geometric heart of the proof is stronger than the eigenvector conclusion. For *any* strictly positive weights $w_j$ summing to one, the weighted barycenter $\mu_w=\sum_jw_jx_j$ satisfies

$$\|x_i-\mu_w\|<\max\{\|x_1-\mu_w\|,\|x_N-\mu_w\|\},\qquad 1<i<N.$$

This follows from the same tail-weight argument; no eigenvector equation enters that part of the proof. The Perron equation then transfers the geometric comparison to the entries of $v$.

For the Gaussian KDE family, the [Fourier proof](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/5_bandwidth_selection.tex:415) makes the directional compatibility concrete:

$$\widehat f_h(\omega)=P_n(\omega)e^{-h^2\omega^2/2},\qquad
\langle\delta_k,\delta_\ell\rangle=\frac1{2\pi}\int |P_n(\omega)|^2b_k(\omega)b_\ell(\omega)\,d\omega.$$

Every multiplier difference $b_k(\omega)$ is negative away from zero. Each increase of bandwidth therefore damps the same Fourier components in a compatible direction. The product is nonnegative, and is positive on a set of positive measure near zero. This is an interpretation of the existing proof. It does not assert pointwise monotonicity of the density values as bandwidth increases.

For spectral scale selection, the practical consequence is exact: using the raw squared $L^2$ divergence selects an extreme bandwidth for every dataset and every strictly increasing finite bandwidth grid. Multiplying $D$ by $1/2$ does not change its eigenvectors. A decreasing similarity transformation removes this particular endpoint theorem as an obstruction; it does not by itself guarantee an interior or statistically optimal choice. These are deductions from the [lemma and proposition](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/5_bandwidth_selection.tex:382).

## 4. Refinement: strict acuteness is unnecessary

This is a further derivation from the existing proof, not a result attributed to the literature.

Assume only $\langle\delta_k,\delta_\ell\rangle\ge0$ and nonzero consecutive increments. For any strictly positive weights $w_j$ summing to one, put $a_k=\sum_{j>k}w_j$. Then $1>a_1>\cdots>a_{N-1}>0$ and, for $1<i<N$,

$$x_i-\mu_w=P_i-Q_i,\qquad
P_i=\sum_{k<i}(1-a_k)\delta_k,\qquad Q_i=\sum_{k\ge i}a_k\delta_k.$$

Because all increment inner products are nonnegative,

$$\|P_i-Q_i\|^2\le\|P_i+Q_i\|^2.$$

The squared norm of a nonnegative increment combination is strictly increasing when any coefficient is increased and all other coefficients are held fixed: its Gram expansion has nonnegative terms and a strictly positive diagonal term for that increment.

If $a_{i-1}>1/2$, then $1-a_k<a_k$ for every $k<i$. Therefore

$$\|P_i+Q_i\|^2<\left\|\sum_ka_k\delta_k\right\|^2=\|\mu_w-x_1\|^2.$$

If $a_{i-1}\le1/2$, then $a_k<a_{i-1}\le1/2$ for every $k\ge i$, so $a_k<1-a_k$ there. Therefore

$$\|P_i+Q_i\|^2<\left\|\sum_k(1-a_k)\delta_k\right\|^2=\|x_N-\mu_w\|^2.$$

Either case yields the same strict endpoint comparison. Setting $w=v$ proves Lemma 5 under the weak condition. Strict positivity remains useful for the stronger strict Monge and strictly superadditive distance statements.

## 5. Limits of the interpretation: two constructed examples

These examples and numerical values were constructed and checked during this investigation; they are not literature claims.

**Robinson alone does not force an endpoint maximum**, even for squared Euclidean distances. Consider

$$D=\begin{pmatrix}
0&.11&1.02&1.13&1.14\\
.11&0&1.01&1.12&1.13\\
1.02&1.01&0&1.01&1.02\\
1.13&1.12&1.01&0&.11\\
1.14&1.13&1.02&.11&0
\end{pmatrix}.$$

Every ordered triple satisfies the strict Robinson inequality. Its normalized Perron vector is approximately

$$v=(.19507603,.19339860,.22305073,.19339860,.19507603)^\top,$$

with its unique maximum at the middle point. The adjacent mixed difference $D_{23}+D_{34}-D_{24}-D_{33}=.90>0$ violates Monge.

An explicit squared-Euclidean realization can be verified as follows. Set $a^2=.2625$, $b^2=.025$, $c^2=.7125$, and

$$y_1=(-a,0,b,0),\ y_2=(-a,0,-b,0),\ y_3=(0,c,0,0),\ y_4=(a,0,0,b),\ y_5=(a,0,0,-b).$$

For orthonormal $e_1,\ldots,e_4$, set $z_i=.1\sum_{r<i}e_r$ and $x_i=(y_i,z_i)$. The distances within the $y$ groups are $.1$, to the middle point are $1$, and between opposite groups are $1.1$. Adding $\|z_i-z_j\|^2=.01|i-j|$ gives precisely $D$ above. Thus the example consists of distinct Euclidean points.

**The acute condition does not force a simple U shape.** Take $x_1=(0,0)$ and successive increments $(40,2),(2,1),(1,6),(6,40)$. Every pair of increments has positive inner product. The Perron vector of their squared-distance matrix, normalized to sum to one, is approximately

$$v=(.28074800,.14666542,.14775257,.13453978,.29029423)^\top.$$

There is a small interior local maximum at coordinate 3, although the global maximum is at coordinate 5. The lemma controls global maximizers; it does not imply monotonicity, symmetry, or absence of interior local maxima.

## 7. Suggested short thesis discussion

The condition that forward increments have pairwise nonnegative inner products has a direct precedent in the geometric interpretation of Monge matrices by Hütter et al. (§1.2, equation (1.2), p. 3052 of the published version). We use its strict version and call the resulting ordered family a strictly acute chain. Geometrically, all forward chords remain positively aligned, although the chain need not be a straight line. Algebraically, the squared-distance matrix is Monge because its adjacent mixed differences equal minus twice the inner products of the increments. This also implies the Robinson inequality. [Hütter et al., published PDF](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/bernoulli/volume-26/issue-4/Estimation-of-Monge-matrices/10.3150/20-BEJ1215.pdf#page=2); [Fiedler, Theorem 2.2](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/133983/MathBohem_127-2002-1_3.pdf).

The endpoint conclusion has related precedents for distance matrices of paths: Corollary 3.2 of Bapat et al., specialized to a path and squared distances, yields an endpoint maximum. Lemma 5 establishes the comparison for the present Hilbert-space geometry. Its proof shows that the Perron entries rank squared distances from their own weighted barycenter. For Gaussian kernel density estimates, increasing bandwidths produce compatible damping of Fourier components and hence satisfy the acute-chain condition. Consequently, the raw squared $L^2$ divergence structurally favors an extreme bandwidth, motivating its conversion into a similarity matrix. [Bapat et al., Corollary 3.2](https://luit.tezu.ernet.in/naac2/Criteria-3/3.7.1/Relevant_Documents/78.pdf); [local lemma, proof, and Gaussian proposition](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/5_bandwidth_selection.tex:382).
