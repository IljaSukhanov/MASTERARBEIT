The recommendations below distinguish published results from proposed applications to the thesis. They are selected for the existing topics: other geometric functionals, general innovation distributions, and real data. The first four are the most useful reading priorities in my assessment.

## 1. KDE estimation of a region with prescribed probability

**Benoit Cadre, Bruno Pelletier and Pierre Pudlo (2013). Estimation of density level sets with a given probability content. Journal of Nonparametric Statistics 25(1), 261–272.**

[Publisher and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10485252.2012.750319) · [Author manuscript](https://pelletierb.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Publications_files/cpp-jnps.pdf). BibTeX: `cadre2013probabilitycontent`.

The authors estimate a density upper-level set with specified probability content using a kernel density estimator and a sample-dependent threshold. They establish a convergence rate for the Lebesgue measure of the symmetric difference. Their sample is i.i.d.; see the abstract in the publisher record and author manuscript.

**Recommended use:** this is a direct match for the missing reference at [outlook line 44](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/999_outlook.tex:44). The estimated threshold should be part of the proposed method, rather than treating the unknown population threshold as available. Establishing a corresponding result for overlapping AR windows remains additional work. Full theorem assumptions were not verified in this search; the recommendation is based on the primary abstract, not an asserted theorem number.

## 2. Why log-concave innovations lead to useful region geometry

**Adrien Saumard and Jon A. Wellner (2014). Log-concavity and strong log-concavity: A review. Statistics Surveys 8, 45–114.**

[Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4847755/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-SS107). BibTeX: `saumardwellner2014logconcavity`.

**Read:** Definition 2.1; Propositions 3.1, 3.2, 3.5 and 3.6; Theorem 2.7 for the relation between full-dimensional log-concave measures and densities. These cover the definition and preservation under linear maps, products, convolution and weak limits.

**Recommended use:** support the [log-concavity paragraph](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/999_outlook.tex:46). Applying these results to convergent finite partial sums of the [window representation](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/3_application_to_time_series.tex:184) is the proposed AR-specific argument. After establishing a full-dimensional log-concave window density, convexity of its positive upper-level sets follows directly from Definition 2.1. This application is our deduction, not a theorem about AR estimation proved in the review.

## 3. A route from region estimation to dependent time series

**Wolfgang Polonik and Qiwei Yao (2000). Conditional Minimum Volume Predictive Regions for Stochastic Processes. Journal of the American Statistical Association 95(450), 509–519.**

[Author repository and manuscript link](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/6311/) · [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2000.10474228). BibTeX: `polonikyao2000minimumvolume`.

The paper constructs conditional minimum-volume predictive regions for strictly stationary processes and gives consistency, rates and asymptotic results under strong mixing and additional assumptions; see the primary repository abstract.

**Recommended use:** add dependence as an explicit theoretical task in the outlook. The thesis uses overlapping windows, whereas Cadre et al. and Scott–Nowak formulate their sampling results for independent observations. Polonik–Yao is a related conditional prediction construction; it does not directly establish convergence of the proposed unconditional window-region estimator or AR coefficient recovery. Detailed theorem hypotheses were not inspected here.

## 4. Concrete formulas for mean width and intrinsic volumes

**Antal Joos and Zsolt Langi (2023). Isoperimetric problems for zonotopes. Mathematika 69(2), 508–534.**

[Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.12191) · [Open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.03204). BibTeX: `joos_isoperimetric_2023`.

**Read:** Section 2, Corollary 1 and Remark 1, manuscript p. 5. Corollary 1 expresses each intrinsic volume of a finite zonotope through sums of volumes of parallelotopes generated by subsets of its generators. Remark 1 gives mean width as a dimension-dependent constant times the total generator length.

**Recommended use:** make the [other-functionals paragraph](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/999_outlook.tex:9) more concrete: analyze these quantities first on the finite zonotope approximations already used in the chapter, and then investigate population limits and empirical estimation. Whether the resulting sequence identifies AR order is a proposed research question. The paper supplies geometric formulas, not that identification result. An arXiv entry already exists in the workspace's `references.bib`; the companion bibliography supplies its journal metadata.

## 5. An alternative to the full support: zonoid-trimmed regions

**Gleb Koshevoy and Karl Mosler (1997). Zonoid trimming for multivariate distributions. The Annals of Statistics 25(5), 1998–2017.**

[Author-hosted full paper](https://wisostat.uni-koeln.de/sites/dep_econometrics/pdf_publikationen/1997_KoshevoyM97.pdf). BibTeX: `koshevoymosler1997zonoidtrimming`.

**Read:** Definition 1.1 for finite-first-moment distributions; Theorem 4.1 for empirical regions; Theorem 5.4 for compactness and convexity at positive trimming levels; Theorem 5.5 for the zero-trimming support limit. Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.1 address continuity and empirical convergence with their stated convergence and sampling conditions.

**Recommended use:** propose studying widths of these regions for window distributions whose full support is unbounded. Their positive-trimming compactness provides a concrete reason to consider them. This is a proposed adaptation to AR inference, whose identifiability and dependent-sample theory remain to be established. Zonoid-trimmed regions are not generally HDRs or prescribed-coverage minimum-volume sets, and their trimming parameter should not be identified with a coverage probability. The finite-first-moment requirement also limits which heavy-tailed cases are covered.

## 6. Real-data motivation with a clear model distinction

**Marco Lauricella and Lorenzo Fagiano (2020). Set Membership Identification of Linear Systems With Guaranteed Simulation Accuracy. IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 65(12), 5189–5204.**

[Open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07628) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2020.2970146). BibTeX: `lauricellafagiano2020setmembership`.

**Read:** Section II, Assumption 2 and equation (2), for bounded measurement noise; Section VI-B for experimental data from an autonomous tethered aircraft used in airborne wind energy.

**Recommended use:** motivate a possible application direction in dynamic-system identification with bounded uncertainty, addressing the [real-data paragraph](/Users/iljasukhanov/MASTERARBEIT/Abschlussarbeit_2021/LaTeX/chapters/999_outlook.tex:64). Its noise is measurement noise and its models include external inputs. It is an adjacent application, not evidence that a real univariate pure AR process has i.i.d. bounded innovations. I did not verify the current wind/solar/traffic innovation claims. Those need specific model evidence before being stated as established applications of this estimator.

## 8. Keep the existing HDR foundation

**Rob J. Hyndman (1996). Computing and Graphing Highest Density Regions. The American Statistician 50(2), 120–126.**

[Author page with paper link](https://robjhyndman.com/publications/computing-and-graphing-highest-density-regions/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.1996.10474359). Existing key: `Hyndman01051996`.

This is the existing methodological foundation for computing and displaying HDRs, including possibly multivariate and multimodal densities; see the author's abstract. Keep it for the HDR introduction, and add Cadre et al. for the specific statistical estimation problem with a data-dependent threshold.

## Suggested emphasis

My recommendation is to make each proposal state its candidate geometric object, the established result motivating it, and the AR-specific question that remains open. In particular, estimating a region consistently and recovering an AR order from that region are separate research tasks. The papers above motivate the objects and estimation methods; they do not establish the proposed AR order-identification results.
