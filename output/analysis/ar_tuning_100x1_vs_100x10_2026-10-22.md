## Interpretation — assessment based on these results

The 100 × 1 contest compares configurations on one particular path per model.
Its mean is just that path's achieved width. The 100 × 10 contest compares the
means of ten achieved widths for each model/configuration. The latter is an
estimate of within-model expected performance under the specified path and
starting-direction experiment. The same number of AR models remains in the
outer comparison.

Lower process-win counts in 100 × 10 do not, by themselves, indicate worse
optimization. Many configurations co-win individual paths. Their relative
achieved widths can differ across paths, so averaging can separate their means;
a configuration can lose a mean contest because of poorer outcomes on some of
the additional paths. Winning a mean contest does not require winning every
individual path.

Both experiments favor an e1, diameter-normalized configuration under their
respective process-win scores. The exact scale and half-life selection changes.
The ten-realization leader wins or co-wins 15 of the 100 sampled models;
this is not evidence that it dominates most models or that its lead will persist
with more realizations or fresh AR models.

The 100 × 10 data also demonstrate that the scoring objectives differ: c=0.3,
H=50 co-wins more individual paths and has a lower overall mean width than
c=0.1, H=100, while c=0.1, H=100 wins more process-level mean contests against
all 288 candidates. Selecting it is supported by the chosen process-win
criterion, not by every possible performance summary.

No confidence interval or test of the leading-count difference was constructed
in this audit. Repetition counts and estimated performance uncertainty need
separate assessment; see [Morris, White and Crowther (2019), Sections 4.3 and 5.3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sim.8086).
