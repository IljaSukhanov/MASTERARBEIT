Why Select One Scale
Persistent homology is scale-agnostic: the persistence diagram records topological features across all filtration scales simultaneously and thereby avoids committing to any
single resolution. Nevertheless, a surprising number of problems in and around TDA reduce to the choice of one filtration parameter , and doing so in a theoretically
justified way remains a longstanding methodological gap. We list four representative settings in which a topologically justified choice of scale is essential:
Reconstruction and meshing from point clouds Topology-aware clustering and graph construction
Statistical inference for point processes Materials and physical systems with intrinsic length scales

In addition to the lens and cover, the output of Mapper depends on the clustering algorithm used on each Xi. Afterwards, each cluster is seen as a node and an edge
between two nodes is formed if the clusters overlap, cf. Figure 5. In some applications, like clinical studies or epidemiological analyses, one wishes to keep the interpretation
of each node as a single subject (patient/county), cf. Figure 6. M αN removes clustering from graph construction, but community detection may still be applied afterward.
The question becomes, by which rule to connect two nodes.
MαN graph approach: Synthetic dataset experiment
Instead of clustering each Xi, connect two points x,y∈X that belong to at least one common pullback set if and only if
d(x,y )≤max{ϵi :i∈I,x,y ∈Xi}
The thresholds ϵi are chosen by applying the scale selection principle to Xi using the functional f(K) =C(K)·E(K). This allows the graph to adapt its scale locally
instead of imposing one global threshold. An exemplary scale selection is visualized in Figure 8. As a first stress test, the M αN approach was tested on a “noisy 8”
dataset.
